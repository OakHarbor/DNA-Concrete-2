## **Common Reasons to Restore Your Pool Deck**

Many folks reach out to us because their pool deck just doesn’t look right anymore. Discoloration is a big one. Stains from leaves, rust marks from patio furniture, and faded spots where sealant wore off can make the whole area feel neglected, even if the concrete underneath is still solid.

Then there’s comfort. When the surface breaks down, it doesn’t feel great on bare feet. Some homeowners describe it as scratchy or chalky. And when smooth sealants or worn finishes get wet, they can be dangerously slick. That’s a real concern if kids or older adults are using the space. In each case, the goal is to make the space safe and enjoyable again.

## **Pool Deck Repair and Restoration Options**

The right fix depends on what shape the concrete is in, how you use the space, and what kind of outcome you want. Below are the main options we offer, along with typical costs for an 800-square-foot deck.

### **Power Wash + (Optional Stain) + Seal**

**Estimated Cost:** $4,800 to $5,500
 **Per Square Foot:** $6.00 to $6.90

This option works when the concrete is still in good shape but just looks tired. We start by power washing to remove grime, algae, and buildup. That alone can make a big difference.

If the color looks washed out or uneven, we can apply a penetrating stain. We often use Ameripolish ColorJuice E, which adds rich, translucent color and soaks into the concrete instead of sitting on top. Stain is optional, but it’s popular with homeowners who want to revive the look without spending a fortune.

Once cleaned and stained, we apply a concrete sealer that blocks moisture and provides a little UV protection. It also helps the deck hold up better against chlorinated water and future staining.

This type of work is best for decks that don’t have traction issues and don’t need physical repairs. It’s a solid refresh when the surface is mostly intact but visually worn.

### **Grind + (Optional Stain, Grip Additive) + Seal**

**Estimated Cost:** $9,000 to $10,000
 **Per Square Foot:** $11.25 to $12.50

If the surface is uneven, pitted, or rough, we usually recommend grinding. This removes the top layer of damaged concrete and creates a smoother base that’s ready for staining and sealing. Grinding is also a great way to remove thin coatings or surface contaminants.

Once ground, the concrete can be stained just like in the first option. From there, we often include a grip additive like silica sand or aluminum oxide. This gives the surface a little texture, which helps reduce slipping without making it uncomfortable to walk on.

After that, the deck is sealed to keep out moisture and reduce wear from sun exposure. This option strikes a balance between cosmetic improvement and functional repair. It’s ideal for decks that are starting to show age but aren’t crumbling or deeply cracked.

### **Westcoat 11-10 System: Epoxy + Acrylic Coating**

**Estimated Cost:** $10,000 to $11,000
 **Per Square Foot:** $12.50 to $13.75

For decks with heavier wear or widespread surface flaws, we install the Westcoat 11-10 resurfacing system. It’s a two-layer solution that transforms the surface completely.

The first step is applying an epoxy base coat. This locks onto the concrete and gives us a smooth, bonded foundation to build on. Then we apply an acrylic texture coat, which adds traction and visual appeal. The finish can be customized with colors or light patterns, depending on your preferences.

This is a good fit when the concrete is cracked, patchy, or uneven, or if you want a consistent, professional look across the entire surface. It’s more of an overhaul than a repair, and it holds up well under heavy use.

## **Frequently Asked Questions Regarding Pool Restoration**

### **What Affects Pool Deck Restoration Cost?**

Every job is a little different. The condition of the existing deck plays a big role. If there are cracks, scaling, or spalling, those areas need to be patched before we can stain or seal anything.

Accessibility matters too. Some backyards are wide open, while others have retaining walls, narrow walkways, or limited entry points. If it takes more time to move equipment in and out, that can increase labor costs.

We also price upgrades separately. Custom stain colors, decorative patterns, or extra slip resistance all add time and materials. But we walk through those details during the estimate so nothing catches you off guard.

### **How Long Does a Pool Restoration Take?**

Most of our pool deck jobs take three to five days. A simple clean-and-seal can wrap up in less than a week. More involved projects like grinding or resurfacing need extra time for curing between steps.

We build a schedule around your availability and the drying time required for each layer. If the weather’s cooperating, the process moves pretty quickly.

### **Does It Make More Sense to Restore or Replace?**

Replacing a concrete pool deck is a big investment. It involves demolition, debris hauling, new concrete pour, and a long cure time before the space can be used again.

Restoring the deck skips all that. You keep the structure that’s already there and improve the surface in a way that feels fresh. Most of our clients choose restoration because it’s faster, less disruptive, and far more affordable.
