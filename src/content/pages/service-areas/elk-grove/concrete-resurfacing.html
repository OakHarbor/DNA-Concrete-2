What Story Does Your Concrete Surface Tell?

Every Floor is Unique

One of the biggest misconceptions about concrete resurfacing is that there's a standard process. In reality, we adjust our approach almost every time we start a new project.

Some slabs have surface damage but very little structural wear. Others have been repaired several times over the years and need a more comprehensive solution. We've also worked on concrete that simply lost its appearance after decades of use but was otherwise in great condition.

Looking at the floor before deciding on a finish usually leads to a better result. It also prevents property owners from spending money on work they may not actually need.

We Start With the Concrete You Already Have

Before we recommend polishing, an overlay, or any decorative finish, we want to understand what we're working with.

We'll inspect the surface, look for cracks or weak areas, check previous repairs, and talk about how the space is used every day. A manufacturing facility, restaurant, showroom, and residential garage all place different demands on a concrete floor, so the finished system should reflect that.

Resurfacing Options

Decorative Concrete Overlays

Concrete overlays have become a popular option because they give us the opportunity to completely change the appearance of an existing slab. You may also hear them called flooring overlays, decorative overlays, or micro-toppings.

We often recommend an overlay when the concrete has too much cosmetic damage to polish by itself but remains structurally sound. Instead of removing the slab, we install a new surface that can be finished in several different ways.

Many commercial renovations use overlays because they create a clean, updated appearance without the disruption of demolition. They're also an excellent choice for larger residential garages, patios, and interior concrete floors that need a fresh start.

Concrete Polishing

Not every resurfacing project requires additional material.

When the existing concrete is in good shape, polishing can completely transform the floor. We grind away imperfections, strengthen the surface with densifiers, and continue refining the concrete until it reaches the desired finish.

Clients often notice two things right away. The floor looks brighter, and it becomes much easier to keep clean. Warehouses and commercial buildings also benefit from the reduction in concrete dust that polished surfaces provide.

Concrete Staining

Concrete staining is one of our favorite ways to add personality to a floor without hiding the natural character of the concrete.

Some clients prefer subtle earth tones that give the surface a warm, natural appearance. Others choose deeper colors that complement their branding or interior design. We'll explain the differences between acid stains and water-based stains so you know what to expect before the project begins.

Concrete Sealing

Once resurfacing is complete, a quality sealer helps protect your investment.

Sealing makes routine cleaning easier and helps guard against stains, moisture, and everyday wear. Depending on the project, sealing may be the final step or part of a larger resurfacing system that includes polishing or decorative staining.

First, We Stabilize Your Existing Surface

Repair Surface Damage First

We repair cracks, pitted areas, spalling, and deteriorated sections before decorative work begins. If the concrete has old repairs that have failed over time, we'll address those as well. Spending time here gives the finished floor a much better chance of looking good and performing well for years.

Restore Expansion Joints

Expansion joints are designed to absorb movement, but they require maintenance just like the rest of the floor. When joint filler wears out, dirt and moisture work their way inside, and the concrete edges begin to break down. We remove failed materials, clean the joints thoroughly, repair damaged edges where necessary, and install products designed for commercial traffic and long-term durability.

FAQs About Concrete Resurfacing

Is Concrete Resurfacing Better Than Replacing a Slab?

It depends on the condition of the concrete. If the slab is structurally sound, resurfacing is often the more practical option. We can repair surface damage, improve the appearance, and extend the life of the floor without the expense and downtime that come with demolition and replacement. During the estimate, we'll inspect the concrete and let you know which approach makes the most sense.

What Types of Concrete Can Be Resurfaced?

We resurface many types of concrete, including warehouse floors, manufacturing facilities, offices, retail spaces, showrooms, garages, patios, pool decks, and workshops. Every slab is a little different, so we'll evaluate its condition before recommending polishing, a decorative overlay, staining, sealing, or another resurfacing method.

Can You Combine Resurfacing With Decorative Finishes?

Yes. In fact, that's one of the advantages of resurfacing. Once the concrete has been repaired or an overlay has been installed, we can polish the surface, apply an acid or water-based stain, and finish it with a protective sealer. This gives property owners much more flexibility than simply repairing the concrete alone.

How Do You Prepare Concrete Before Resurfacing?

Preparation is one of the biggest factors in how well a resurfacing project performs over time. We typically grind the surface to remove old coatings and contaminants, repair cracks or damaged areas, restore expansion joints when necessary, and clean the slab thoroughly before any decorative work begins. A properly prepared surface gives the finished floor the best chance to last.
