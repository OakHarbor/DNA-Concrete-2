How Do You Need Your Floor to Perform?

Forklifts, carts, vehicles, machinery, and constant foot traffic wear concrete in very specific ways. We often see tire marks, rough travel lanes, chipped joint edges, oil stains, surface dust, and patched areas that have started to break down again.

A cosmetic refresh is likely not good enough in your use-case. We look at how the slab is used every day and choose a resurfacing method that can handle the traffic without creating unnecessary maintenance later.

Decorative Spaces

Showrooms, offices, restaurants, patios, and customer-facing spaces usually give us more room to focus on appearance.

Polished concrete can create a clean, finished look without covering the slab. Staining adds color while keeping some of the concrete's natural variation. Decorative overlays can completely change a worn surface when the original floor no longer has the look you want.

Resurface Your Concrete With the Right Finish

Polish the Slab

If the existing concrete is in decent condition, polishing may be all it needs.

We grind the surface with progressively finer abrasives, repair problem areas, and refine the concrete to the finish you choose. A densifier may also be used to strengthen the surface. The finished floor is easier to clean, produces less dust, and holds up well in commercial spaces.

Polishing works especially well in warehouses, offices, retail buildings, showrooms, and other interiors where owners want durability without a thick coating on top.

Install a Concrete Overlay

When the visible surface is too rough, patched, stained, or uneven to polish attractively, an overlay can give us a better starting point.

Concrete overlays, flooring overlays, and micro-toppings create a new surface over the existing slab. We can then polish, stain, seal, or otherwise finish that surface depending on the project.

This can be a strong option for commercial renovations, garages, restaurants, retail spaces, and interior floors where the slab itself is still usable but the surface needs a bigger change.

Change the Look With Concrete Staining

Concrete stain works well when you want color without hiding the character of the floor.

Acid-based stains react with minerals in the concrete and tend to create more natural variation. Water-based stains offer a broader color range and more control over the finished appearance.

We can apply stain to suitable existing concrete or incorporate it into a larger resurfacing project.

Seal the Finished Concrete

Sealing helps protect resurfaced concrete from stains, moisture, dust, and everyday wear.

The right sealer depends on the floor and the finish underneath it. Some clients prefer a more natural look. Others want more sheen or stronger surface protection. We'll recommend the product around the way the space is used.

Repair The Base

Fix Cracks, Spalling, Pits, and Old Patches

That may include cracks, pitting, spalled areas, old patchwork, failed coatings, or sections where the surface has become weak. We may grind the floor, remove loose material, repair problem areas, and clean away contaminants before the new finish goes down.

There is no benefit in making damaged concrete look good for a short time. The repairs underneath need to support the finished surface.

Replace Failed Joint Material

Expansion joints take plenty of abuse in busy commercial buildings. Old filler can crack, shrink, or pull away from the edges. Dirt and moisture then collect inside the joint, and rolling equipment can start breaking down the surrounding concrete.

We remove failed material, clean the joint, repair damaged edges when needed, and install a suitable replacement filler before finishing the floor.

Garages, workshops, patios, pool decks, and larger interior concrete floors can also be resurfaced.

We'll look at the concrete and explain which options fit the space without trying to force every project into the same system.
