Commercial and Residential Concrete Staining Services

When is Staining the Right Fit for Your Surface?

Stained concrete holds up under heavy use, so it's a great choice for restaurants, retail stores, showrooms, and office spaces. Business owners who want a more customized look without the upkeep of tile, wood, or carpet often choose staining for its durability and easy maintenance.

Restaurants and breweries often choose concrete staining to create a warm, inviting atmosphere while preventing stains from spills and cleaning chemicals. Retail stores and showrooms benefit from the unique color variations and polished look that stained concrete provides. Many offices and open workspaces use stained concrete in neutral or muted tones to create a modern setting that boosts productivity for their workforce.

Homeowners love staining because of the dimension it adds to garages, patios, basements, and entryways. It offers an alternative to standard gray concrete while requiring very little maintenance. With the right stain, a garage floor takes on a more polished look while resisting tire marks and stains. A stained patio or walkway holds up well under the elements and stays looking clean with minimal upkeep.

How Does a Stain Differ From Polish or Epoxy?

Concrete stains change the color of the surface without covering up its natural texture. Unlike epoxy, which creates a thick protective layer, stains penetrate the concrete, providing color without a glossy, coated appearance.

Polished concrete smooths and refines the surface, but it doesn't add color on its own. Staining can be used along with polishing for a decorative finish, or it can be left with a more natural matte or satin appearance. Epoxy provides a more protective barrier, making it better suited for areas exposed to chemicals, moisture, or heavy impact.

Choose the Right Concrete Stain

Acid-based stains react with the minerals in concrete, creating deep, varied tones that resemble natural stone or marble. This option works well for showrooms, patios, and decorative commercial spaces where a unique, high-end look is preferred.

Water-based stains provide a more even color and come in a wider range of shades, including blues, greens, and modern industrial tones. This option is a great fit for retail stores, offices, and garages where a more uniform color is desired.

The Professional Concrete Stain Application Process

What Surface Prep is Required?

Before applying a stain, the concrete surface must be free of coatings, dirt, or previous treatments. A thorough cleaning and grinding process removes contaminants and creates a surface that allows the stain to penetrate evenly. Cracks or imperfections are repaired before staining to prevent uneven color absorption.

For commercial properties with existing coatings, any old sealants or finishes are removed to allow the stain to fully bond with the concrete. Without this step, the color may not absorb properly, leading to an uneven or faded appearance.

Stain Application and Curing Process

After preparing the surface, the stain is applied using sprayers, rollers, or sponges to create the desired look. Some stains require multiple layers to achieve deeper color saturation or unique textures. Acid-based stains take time to react with the concrete, while water-based stains dry more quickly and can be sealed sooner.

A protective sealer is applied to lock in the color and add resistance to foot traffic, spills, and moisture. Depending on the type of stain used, curing takes between 24 to 48 hours. Once fully cured, the surface is ready for use and requires only minimal maintenance to keep it looking its best.
