Concrete Eventually Shows Its Age

Small Stains and Cracks Come First

You may notice stains that never seem to come out, patches that don't quite match the surrounding floor, or areas that have become rough from years of traffic. Sometimes it's a coating that has started to peel. Other times, the surface begins producing more dust than it used to.

None of those issues should be ignored, but they also shouldn't convince you that replacement is your only option. We look at floors like this all the time, and in many cases the concrete underneath still has plenty of life left.

Over Time, a Worn-Out Surface Makes Your Whole Space Look Shabby

In a warehouse, damaged concrete creates more maintenance and makes the floor harder to keep clean. In a showroom or office, worn concrete can leave visitors with the wrong impression before they've even had a conversation with your staff. At home, a garage or patio that once looked great gradually becomes a space you stop paying attention to.

Resurfacing gives you an opportunity to change that. Instead of working around the flaws, we improve the surface so it looks better, functions better, and is easier to maintain.

Which Type of Repair is Best For You?

Restore the Existing Concrete With Polishing

The good news is that some of the best resurfacing projects involve very little added material! When the slab is in good condition, polishing can completely change its appearance. We grind away minor imperfections, strengthen the surface with densifiers, and continue refining the concrete until it reaches the finish you've chosen.

The result is a floor that looks brighter, stays cleaner, and stands up well to daily use. Warehouses, offices, retail spaces, and commercial buildings often benefit from polished concrete because it combines durability with relatively low maintenance.

Install a Decorative Concrete Overlay

If the surface has too much cosmetic damage, a decorative overlay often makes more sense. Overlays, which are also called flooring overlays or micro-toppings, create a new surface over the existing concrete without removing the slab itself.

Once the overlay is installed, we can polish it, stain it, seal it, or create another decorative finish depending on the project. It's a great option for commercial renovations, retail spaces, restaurants, garages, and many other properties where appearance matters.

Finish the Floor With Staining or Sealing

Some clients prefer a polished gray floor that highlights the natural character of the concrete. Others want decorative staining to introduce color and warmth into the space. Nearly every resurfacing project benefits from a quality sealer that protects the finished surface from moisture, stains, and everyday wear.

We'll talk through those options together so the finished floor fits the building instead of following a one-size-fits-all formula.

Don't Skip the Preparation

Repair Surface Damage First

Before we think about polishing, overlays, or decorative finishes, we repair cracks, pitted areas, spalling, and damaged sections of concrete. If old coatings have failed, we'll remove them so the new finish has a solid foundation to bond with.

It isn't the part of the project anyone gets excited about, but it's one of the reasons a professionally resurfaced floor lasts longer.

Expansion Joint Repair Matters Too

Expansion joints help concrete move as temperatures change and buildings settle over time. Once the joint material begins to fail, moisture and debris work their way inside. Before long, the edges start breaking down, particularly in warehouses and commercial buildings with rolling equipment.

We repair expansion joints before finishing the floor so the surrounding concrete has the support it needs for years to come.
